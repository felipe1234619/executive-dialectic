A pilot in Chile

Six of ten interviewed buyers expressed interest, but the sample came from a single industry association and yielded no signed commitments. A partner estimated the pilot at USD 45,000, excluding local support and contract review.

Finance director’s dissent. A pilot could strain the support team. The finance director called for a cost ceiling and signed customer commitments before any expansion.

What triggered the review?

The partner withdrew its estimate in a written notice. With the original cost basis gone, the committee could no longer assess whether the pilot would fit within its ceiling. The notice did not establish what the pilot would actually cost.

The decision before and after

The fictional executive committee authorized both versions. The revised record refers back to the original decision and preserves the finance director’s dissent.

Original decision

Judgment
Approve a limited pilot through a local partner, subject to a fully costed plan within the USD 60,000 ceiling.
Basis
Initial buyer interest justified testing demand. The partner’s estimate supported further planning, with support and contract costs still to verify.
Review trigger
Review after 90 days, or sooner if the cost basis changes.

Revised decision

Judgment
Suspend the pilot and defer expansion. Procurement may gather quotes, while implementation and new commitments are paused.
Basis
The partner’s written withdrawal removed the cost estimate on which the pilot plan depended.
Review trigger
Reopen when two comparable quotes cover local support, contract review and invoicing.

Conditions that carry forward. The USD 60,000 ceiling still applies to any pilot. Signed customer commitments are still required before expansion.

Who reopens the decision? The procurement lead checks that both quotes meet the review criteria and asks the executive committee to reconvene.
